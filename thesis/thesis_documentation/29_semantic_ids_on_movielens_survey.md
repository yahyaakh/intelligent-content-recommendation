# 29 — Semantic IDs on MovieLens: what others did, and what we can learn

**Status:** literature and code search (2026-10-29).

**How the facts were checked:** read from the arXiv pages and the repository READMEs. Numbers come from the papers' or READMEs' own tables, read through the pages. Re-check any number in the PDF before citing it in the report.

**Context:**
- doc 27 (our IDs, uses 1 and 2);
- doc 28 (use 3: semantic IDs did not help our two-tower or generative models);

## 1. Summary

- **Very little work runs semantic IDs on MovieLens.** Most semantic-ID papers use Amazon (Beauty, Sports, Toys), Yelp or Steam. I found 2 papers and 1 serious code project with MovieLens recommendation results, plus 1 paper with a small MovieLens side experiment.
- **None of them studies our task.** They all predict the *next* film from a long history (leave-one-out, about 165 films per user on ML-1M). Ours is new users with 3–10 seed films. So no number can be compared directly with ours.
- **The most useful lesson for us** comes from Spotify (Penha et al., RecSys 2025, MovieLens 25M). For recommendation, semantic IDs built from **collaborative-filtering embeddings** beat IDs built from **content** embeddings by a wide margin: Recall@30 0.062 vs 0.026. Our IDs are built from content embeddings.
- **The evidence on "TIGER vs SASRec on ML-1M" contradicts itself.** One paper reports TIGER above SASRec. A careful reproduction finds SASRec at about twice the generative model's score. The training loss and the evaluation protocol decide the outcome.

## 2. The works

### 2.1 Penha et al., "Semantic IDs for Joint Generative Search and Recommendation" (Spotify, RecSys 2025 Late-Breaking Results)
[arXiv 2508.10478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10478)

- **Data:**
  - MovieLens 25M, reduced to 62,138 films and 1.24M interactions;
  - chronological split: each user's last interaction is the test item;
  - 20 search queries per film, generated by Gemini-2.0-flash.
- **IDs:** RQ-KMeans (FAISS residual quantiser), **2 levels × 256 codes**. The IDs were built from four kinds of embedding:
  - content (all-mpnet-base-v2 on metadata);
  - search-tuned bi-encoder;
  - **recommendation (ENMF collaborative filtering, 256-d)**;
  - multi-task.
- **Model:** flan-t5-base generating IDs, with diverse beam search (beam 60). Metric: Recall@30, 5 seeds, paired t-tests.
- **Results (Recall@30):**

  | IDs built from | Recommendation | Search |
  |---|---|---|
  | CF embeddings | **0.062** | 0.004 |
  | multi-task | 0.049 | 0.046 |
  | search-tuned (content-like) | 0.026 | 0.072 |

- **Also:** RQ-KMeans beat RQ-VAE and other tokenisers in their ablation.
- **Meaning for us:**
  - This is the strongest evidence that **the embedding the IDs are built from matters more than the quantiser**, and that collaborative embeddings are what recommendation needs.
  - It supports our residual k-means choice.
  - It suggests building IDs from EASE/SVD item vectors (doc 28 §6, option 3).

### 2.2 "Beyond Uniform Token Training: … Token-Weighted Objectives in Generative Recommenders" (2026)
[arXiv 2601.17787](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17787)

- **Data:** MovieLens-1M (6,040 users, 3,883 films), leave-one-out.
- **IDs:** RQ-VAE, 4 × 256, from LLaMA2-7B item embeddings. Collisions resolved by changing the last token.
- **Results (full ranking, Hit@10 / NDCG@10):**
  - SASRec: 0.190 / 0.090;
  - GRU4Rec: 0.181 / 0.084;
  - TIGER: 0.265 / 0.153;
  - TIGER with their token weighting: 0.273 / 0.161 (+3% / +5%).
- **Caveats:**
  - Two tables give different TIGER numbers (0.2648 vs 0.2658).
  - Their SASRec is weak compared with the reproduction in 2.3.
- **Meaning for us:** a published "TIGER beats SASRec on MovieLens" result. It is useful as context, but it depends on how strong the SASRec baseline is.

### 2.3 jeannineshiu/sequential-rec-from-sasrec-to-tiger (GitHub, MIT licence)
[repository](https://github.com/jeannineshiu/sequential-rec-from-sasrec-to-tiger)

- A careful reproduction: one backbone, one evaluation harness, seed-noise measured, README tables checked automatically against the runs.
- **Data:** ML-1M (6,040 users, 3,416 films after 5-core filtering), leave-one-out.
- **IDs:** all-MiniLM-L6-v2 on title + genres; **residual k-means, 3 × 256 + collision token**; 1.46% collisions, no dead codes. The author reports RQ-VAE "was not needed".
- **Model:** "GenRec" = the SASRec backbone with a decoder over ID tokens and trie-constrained decoding, so the item representation is the only difference. This is the same design idea as our doc 28.
- **Results (test, k = 10):**

  | Model | Full-catalog HR@10 | Full-catalog NDCG@10 | Sampled HR@10 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Popularity | 0.037 | 0.018 | 0.436 |
  | BPR-MF | 0.067 | 0.033 | 0.575 |
  | SASRec | **0.248** | **0.132** | 0.819 |
  | GenRec (semantic IDs) | 0.116 | 0.061 | 0.626 |

- **Findings:**
  - Semantic IDs scored **53% lower** than atomic IDs on the full catalog.
  - The seed noise of GenRec is about 3–5% relative.
  - Sampled metrics hide large differences.
  - SASRec's training loss alone (BCE vs full softmax) changes full-catalog HR@10 by about 23%.
  - "Semantic IDs compress a catalog, not a model."
- **Meaning for us:**
  - This is the closest published set-up to our doc 28, and it reaches **the same conclusion**: with everything else fixed, semantic IDs lose to atomic IDs on MovieLens.
  - Our result is not an outlier, and it can be cited as agreeing with independent work.
  - It also shows that the atomic baseline must be trained well (full softmax).

### 2.4 Choi et al., "Quantizing Intent: Cross-Domain Semantic IDs from Organic Activity for Industrial Ranking" (2026)
[arXiv 2606.01396](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01396)

- Mainly an industrial ads paper. It has a **MovieLens-100K side experiment**: predict whether a held-out user–film pair is rated ≥ 4.
- **Unusual design:** the IDs are for **users**, not films.
  - The user embedding is the mean of the user's liked-film text embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, title + genres);
  - it is quantised (3 levels × 256 codes) and fed to an MLP.
- **AUC:**

  | Model | AUC |
  |---|---|
  | No user signal | 0.769 |
  | Dense user embedding | 0.808 |
  | RQ-KMeans user ID | 0.822 |
  | RQ-FSQ user ID | 0.834 |

- **Caveat:** if the liked films used to build the user embedding include the held-out film, this leaks the answer. The page does not say how this was avoided. Do not rely on the numbers.
- **Meaning for us:** an idea, not evidence. A new user's seed films could be summarised as a "user semantic ID", which is a coarse taste group. This is low priority.

### 2.5 Tokeniser-only projects (no recommendation results)

- [EdoardoBotta/RQ-VAE-Recommender](https://github.com/EdoardoBotta/RQ-VAE-Recommender) (MIT):
  - a TIGER implementation (RQ-VAE with k-means initialisation, T5 retrieval model);
  - MovieLens 1M / 32M are supported **for the tokeniser only**, and retrieval training is "not currently exposed";
  - no published numbers.
- [kykamath/movielens-sids](https://github.com/kykamath/movielens-sids) (no licence):
  - RQ-VAE 4 × 1,024 on MovieLens-32M films (all-mpnet-base-v2), plus a UMAP plot;
  - no recommender. Discussed earlier in this session.

### 2.6 Method papers that matter for our next tests (not MovieLens)

| Paper | Finding | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| ["How Reliable Are Semantic-ID Tokenizer Comparisons…" (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25330v1); Amazon + Yelp | When several items share an ID, "SID-level" hits count the whole group. This inflated Hit@10 by up to 103%, and tokeniser rankings flipped under item-level metrics. Residual k-means had the most collisions. | Our generative model already scores real films (collision token + pool trie), so our numbers are item-level. State this in the report, together with our collision rates. |
| ["Can Generative Recommendation Reach Cold Items?" (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21101) | ID-based generators can reach new items only if those items' tokens and prefixes were seen in training. Unseen tokens or paths are hard to reach. | The new-item test must check whether hidden films' IDs use seen prefixes. Report seen vs unseen paths separately. |
| ["Purely Semantic Indexing…" (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16446) | The extra collision token is non-semantic, so it cannot be predicted for unseen items. | In the new-item test, a hidden film's collision token is arbitrary. Either drop it for new items (score at the 3-token level) or use their purely semantic indexing. |
| [Singh et al. (RecSys 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08121), [reproducibility study (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24430), [GRID handbook (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22224) | Already in doc 19. GRID and the reproducibility study also report residual k-means as competitive with or better than RQ-VAE. | Supports our quantiser choice. |

## 3. How our work compares

| | Others on MovieLens | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Task | next item from a long history (leave-one-out) | new users with 3 or 10 seed films |
| Catalog | 3.4k–62k films | 1,549 rated films |
| Codebooks | 2–4 levels × 256 | 3 levels × 32 (+ collision token) |
| ID source | content text (MiniLM, mpnet, LLaMA) or CF (Spotify) | content (embeddinggemma + LLM labels) |
| Baselines | SASRec, GRU4Rec, BPR-MF, popularity | popularity, item-CF, user-kNN, EASE, NCF, SVD, LTR v2, LLM rankers |
| Result for semantic vs atomic IDs | mixed: TIGER > weak SASRec (2.2); atomic ≫ semantic with a strong SASRec (2.3) | atomic > semantic in both models (doc 28) |

**Safe statement for the report:** "On MovieLens-1M, an independent reproduction that kept the backbone fixed also found that semantic IDs scored well below atomic IDs (jeannineshiu, GitHub). Penha et al. found that semantic IDs built from collaborative embeddings recommended far better than those built from content embeddings." Cite the repository as software, not as a peer-reviewed result.

## 4. What could give us better semantic-ID results (ordered by evidence and cost)

1. **Build the IDs from collaborative embeddings** (evidence: Penha et al.). Feed the EASE item vectors (rows of B) or the SVD item vectors (already in G5) into `Semantic_IDs.fit()` in place of the content embedding, then re-run:
   - the meaningfulness check (it will drop for genre and director, which is expected);
   - use 1 (prefix similarity);
   - use 2 (LTR features);
   - the doc 28 models.

   It is cheap: the `Semantic_IDs` class takes any vectors. A hybrid (concatenated content + CF vectors, normalised) is a natural third variant.
2. **Train the generative and two-tower models properly before comparing representations** (evidence: 2.3). Our atomic models are below popularity (doc 28 §4.7). Fixes:
   - use every next-film position of every user, not 10 examples per user and epoch;
   - full-softmax loss over the pool (already used);
   - a SASRec-style Transformer in place of the GRU;
   - a popularity bias term.

   The target is for the atomic version to clear popularity (0.563) before comparing it with semantic IDs.
3. **Codebook size.** Others use 256 codes on 3–4k films with about 1.5% collisions. We use 32 with 3 levels. A grid of {16, 32, 64, 128} on the simulated users is cheap for use 1 and use 2 (doc 27 harness).
4. **New-item test, designed after 2.6:**
   - hide about 10% of pool films from training, chosen at random with a fixed seed;
   - give them IDs from the k-means fitted on all films (the content is known for new films);
   - score at the 3-token level, without the collision token;
   - report item-level hits on hidden films, split by whether the hidden film's prefix was seen in training.
5. **User semantic IDs** (idea from 2.4): quantise the mean seed embedding into a "taste group" and use it as an LTR feature. Low priority, and the leakage check comes first.

Rules as before: settings chosen on simulated users, 3 runs, 203 validation users, and the decision rule fixed in advance.
